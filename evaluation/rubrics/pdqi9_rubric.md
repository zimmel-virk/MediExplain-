# PDQI-9 Rubric — Clinical Note Quality

**For doctor reviewers**

You will be shown N AI-generated SOAP notes from MediExplain+. For each note,
score the nine dimensions below on a 1–5 scale. There is no right answer for
each dimension — give your honest clinical judgement.

The notes are anonymised — patient names and identifying details have been
removed. Please do not attempt to identify any patient.

---

## Scoring scale (applies to every dimension)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strongly disagree / very poor |
| 2 | Disagree / poor |
| 3 | Neutral / acceptable |
| 4 | Agree / good |
| 5 | Strongly agree / excellent |

---

## The nine dimensions

For each note, score each dimension 1–5:

1. **Up-to-date** — reflects all the relevant findings discussed
2. **Accurate** — no factual errors, no clinical contradictions
3. **Thorough** — covers all relevant aspects of the consultation
4. **Useful** — would be clinically helpful in continuing care
5. **Organised** — follows logical SOAP structure
6. **Concise** — no redundant or filler content
7. **Consistent** — internal consistency (same patient, no contradictions)
8. **Succinct** — appropriately brief for the consultation length
9. **Internally consistent** — terminology and abbreviations used consistently

## After scoring

Hand the sheets back to the student researcher. They will:

1. Compute mean ± standard deviation per dimension across all reviewers
2. Compute overall total score distribution
3. Compute Krippendorff's α to measure inter-rater agreement
4. Catalogue any 1- or 2-scored notes for failure-mode analysis

You will receive a summary of the results once compiled.

## Source

This rubric is the standard Physician Documentation Quality Instrument
(PDQI-9), widely used in clinical informatics for evaluating notes whether
human- or AI-generated. See Stetson et al. (2008, 2012) for the original
validation work.
